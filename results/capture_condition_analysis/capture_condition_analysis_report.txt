# TerraSpectra
# Capture Condition Analysis Report

## 1. Introduction

This analysis investigates how image acquisition conditions may influence RGB image features in the TerraSpectra tomato hyperspectral dataset.

The main acquisition parameters examined were:

- Imaging distance
- Integration time
- Weather conditions

The image features examined were:

- Red channel mean
- Green channel mean
- Blue channel mean
- Overall image brightness

The purpose was to identify potential acquisition-related variations that should be considered when interpreting temporal image features.

## 2. Dataset Overview

The analysis used the Trial 4 plant-level temporal dataset.

| Description | Value |
|---|---:|
| Original image records | 247 |
| Plant-date observations | 71 |
| Unique plants | 11 |
| Group 028 observations | 28 |
| Group 065 observations | 28 |
| Control group K observations | 15 |
| Image features examined | 4 |
| Acquisition parameters | 2 |

The three groups represent two inoculated plant groups and one control group.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Data preparation

The plant-level temporal dataset was loaded from:

results/plant_level_temporal/plant_daily_summary.csv

The dataset contained image features and acquisition metadata.

### 3.2 Statistical analysis

Spearman rank correlation was used to examine monotonic associations between acquisition parameters and image features.

The analysis was performed for:

- All observations combined.
- Group 028.
- Group 065.
- Control group K.

The following parameters were examined:

- Imaging distance versus RGB means and brightness.
- Integration time versus RGB means and brightness.

A nominal p-value threshold of 0.05 was used for initial exploratory interpretation.

### 3.3 Weather analysis

Weather descriptions were normalized into three categories:

- Sunny
- Cloudy
- Partly Cloudy

Mean and median brightness were calculated for each category.

### 3.4 Visualization

Nine graphs were generated:

- Four acquisition parameter versus image brightness/RGB relationships for imaging distance and integration time.
- One weather versus brightness graph.

Specifically, the outputs include eight scatter plots and one weather comparison graph.

## 6. Multiple-Testing Consideration

A total of 24 group-wise correlation tests were conducted.

Using a Bonferroni correction:

0.05 / 24 = 0.002083

Neither of the two nominally significant group-wise findings met this stricter threshold.

Therefore, they should be treated as exploratory observations rather than confirmed statistical findings.

## 7. Weather and Brightness Analysis

| Weather Condition | Observations | Mean Brightness | Median Brightness |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cloudy | 27 | 61.365 | 57.838 |
| Partly Cloudy | 33 | 66.587 | 64.766 |
| Sunny | 11 | 56.531 | 56.722 |

Partly cloudy conditions had the highest average brightness, while sunny conditions had the lowest.

The difference between partly cloudy and sunny mean brightness was approximately 10.06 units.

These results describe differences in observed brightness across weather categories. They do not establish that weather caused the differences.

## 8. Key Findings

1. Integration time showed negative pooled associations with all four RGB-related image features.

2. Red-channel intensity showed the strongest pooled association with integration time.

3. Imaging distance showed its strongest pooled association with blue-channel intensity.

4. Weather categories showed different average image brightness values.

5. Group-wise relationships differed in strength and direction.

6. Acquisition conditions should be considered when interpreting temporal RGB changes.

## 9. Limitations

1. The dataset contains 71 plant-date observations but only 11 unique plants.

2. Repeated observations from the same plant are not independent biological replicates.

3. The current Spearman correlation analysis does not account for repeated measurements within plants.

4. Group-wise sample sizes are relatively small: 28 observations for groups 028 and 065, and 15 for K.

5. Weather categories have unequal sample sizes.

6. Acquisition parameters and weather conditions may vary together.

7. The control group has different observation dates from the inoculated groups, limiting direct same-day comparisons.

8. RGB colour and brightness measurements may also be affected by lighting, background, image framing and other capture conditions.

9. No validated disease-severity labels were used in this analysis.

10. These results cannot establish causal relationships or disease severity.

## 10. Recommendations

1. Preserve imaging distance, integration time and weather metadata during future analysis.

2. Investigate acquisition-condition normalization before comparing temporal RGB features.

3. Consider mixed-effects statistical models to account for repeated measurements within plants.

4. Use plant identity as a grouping factor in future statistical analyses.

5. Collect additional observations from independent plants.

6. Where possible, standardize imaging distance and camera acquisition settings.

7. Record consistent weather and lighting information.

8. Validate any proposed image correction method before applying it to biological interpretation.

## 11. Conclusion

The capture-condition analysis identified several associations between image acquisition parameters and RGB image features in the TerraSpectra dataset.

Integration time showed negative pooled associations with red, green, blue and overall brightness measurements. Imaging distance showed a positive association with blue-channel intensity. Mean brightness also differed across weather categories.

The group-wise analysis demonstrated that these relationships were not consistent across all plant groups.

These findings indicate that acquisition conditions are important considerations when interpreting temporal RGB measurements.

However, because the dataset contains repeated observations from a small number of plants, the results should be considered exploratory. Further analysis using methods that account for repeated measurements and additional independent plant samples is recommended.

The analysis does not establish disease severity or validated disease forecasting performance.
